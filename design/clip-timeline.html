Clip timeline ideas

The clipper's timeline today always shows the whole video, so on a long video or a one-hour live window a 30-second clip is a sliver between two fat handles. Here are three other ways to do it, each one you can actually drag around. The sample is a 1:32:40 video; the last one is a live stream.

Today

The whole video, always

What Neria does now. Try to make a 20-second clip at 51:10: you can't see it, and the handles cover it.

Idea A

Zoom and scroll

Like a video editor. Zoom in to seconds, scroll sideways along the strip, and a thin overview on top shows where you are (drag the window on it). Handles are thin lines with a grip, the clip is a band, and time marks adapt to the zoom.

Idea B

Overview and close-up

Two strips. The small one is always the whole video: click or drag on it to pick where you are. The big one is a close-up around your clip, where the trimming happens. Pick how close with the chips; there's no zooming to fiddle with.

Idea C

Live tape

For live streams. Time reads backwards from now, the live edge sits on the right and keeps moving, and you see the last five minutes by default; scroll left to go back as far as the site keeps (an hour on YouTube). "Last 30 s" drops a clip at the edge you can then nudge.

A · Zoom and scroll
  • Most powerful, familiar from editors
  • Zooming is one more thing to learn
  • Works for files, links and lives alike
B · Overview and close-up
  • No zoom to manage: two fixed views
  • Precise trimming in the close-up
  • Costs a little more height
C · Live tape
  • Only for live streams
  • Reads "how long ago", not a position
  • Pairs with A or B for everything else
